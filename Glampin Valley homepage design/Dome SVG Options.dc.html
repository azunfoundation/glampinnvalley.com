Six ways to draw the dome

Each is a single-stroke line drawing in brand gold on cream and on forest green, so you can judge it in both chapters. Pick one (or say what to blend) and I'll build the new section around it.

Dome on the deck1a

Full geodesic shell with the round valley window, on a railed deck with two stargazer chairs. Closest to the real property.

Dome under the stars1b

Simplified silhouette with only a few struts, a crescent moon and a scatter of stars. Softer, more poetic; good for the night chapters.

Dome in the trees1c

The dome half-hidden behind foliage and grasses, with a hillline behind. Emphasises the forest setting over the structure.

Twin domes on the ridge1d

Two domes on a shared deck with the valley falling away below, the path between them and a setting sun. A wide, panoramic composition.

Dome section (cutaway)1e

Architectural half-section: the shell cut open to show the bed, side table and window, with the hammock hung out over the drop. Suits a "what's inside" section.

Dome monogram1f

A compact emblem: dome, ridgeline and a single star inside a circle. Works small — as a section marker, bullet, or watermark repeated as a pattern.
